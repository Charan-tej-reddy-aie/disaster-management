Abstract—Disaster management requires coordination between
disaster identification, impact assessment, emergency resource
estimation, authorization, allocation, and movement tracking. In
many existing workflows, these activities are handled through
separate systems, which can make it difficult to connect disaster
information with operational resource decisions. This paper
presents an intelligent cloud-based disaster management system
that integrates disaster analysis, impact assessment, resource
requirement estimation, hierarchical approval, quantum-inspired
resource allocation, and blockchain-based tracking within a
unified framework. The system combines historical disaster
records, meteorological information, and satellite observations
to support disaster analysis. The resulting information is used
to estimate emergency requirements and initiate a structured
approval workflow involving state and national authorities. After
authorization, a quantum-inspired multi-warehouse optimization
service generates feasible allocation plans by considering re-
source demand, warehouse availability, priority, and geographical
distance. Allocated resources are subsequently tracked through
planning, dispatch, transit, and delivery stages. Selected move-
ment events are additionally recorded through a blockchain-
based audit layer. The implementation combines React and Vite,
Node.js and Express, MySQL, Python-based services, FastAPI,
and Copernicus satellite-data services. The proposed framework
therefore connects disaster intelligence with resource planning,
approval, constrained allocation, and auditable logistics in a
common cloud-oriented environment.

I. INTRODUCTION
Natural disasters such as floods, cyclones, landslides, earth-
quakes, wildfires, and extreme weather events can cause
significant disruption to communities, infrastructure, trans-
portation, and essential services. Effective disaster response
requires rapid access to reliable information and coordinated
decisions involving impact assessment, resource requirements,
authorization, allocation, and subsequent monitoring. Recent
research has investigated different technological approaches
for improving disaster response, including UA V-supported
edge computing, blockchain-based resilience networks, intelli-
gent resource management, and digital-twin-based emergency
monitoring [1]–[5].
A major challenge in disaster management is the fragmen-
tation of information and operational activities across different
organizations and systems. Blockchain-based approaches have
therefore been explored for disaster management, emergency
coordination, resource sharing, and aid distribution [6]–[8].
Recent reviews have also examined blockchain applications
in emergency-management systems and identified continuing
research opportunities [9], [10]. Other studies have considered
blockchain frameworks for earthquake risk mitigation, disaster
prevention, and relief operations [11], [12].
The increasing availability of historical disaster records, me-
teorological observations, satellite imagery, and cloud-based
computing services provides an opportunity to integrate disas-
ter intelligence within a common platform. Satellite observa-
tions provide an additional spatial perspective for assessing

Emergency resource distribution represents another impor-
tant challenge in disaster response. Resources must be supplied
according to demand while considering available inventory,
warehouse locations, priority, transportation distance, and
other operational constraints. Optimization techniques have
therefore been widely studied in emergency logistics, includ-
ing relief distribution and post-disaster resource decisions [16].
Recent work has also explored quantum-adaptive resource
management and quantum-inspired approaches in edge and
disaster-related environments [1], [4]. The use of quantum and
post-quantum concepts has additionally been investigated in
secure distributed systems, including post-quantum blockchain
consensus and quantum-geospatial disaster platforms [15],
[17]. However, post-quantum security and quantum optimiza-
tion address different technical objectives; the proposed system
uses a classical quantum-inspired optimization implementation
and does not claim execution on quantum hardware.
Blockchain has also been investigated as an audit and
traceability mechanism in humanitarian operations. Previous
research has examined its use for information management,
secure data sharing, transparency, and resource-related trans-
actions [18], [19]. IoT-enabled blockchain systems have fur-
ther been proposed for disaster monitoring, resilience, secure
data storage, and recovery [20]. Blockchain-enabled secure
transaction models have also been studied in other resource
and energy domains, illustrating the broader use of distributed
ledgers and smart contracts for traceable transactions [21].
Based on these research directions, this paper presents an
intelligent cloud-based disaster management system that con-
nects disaster analysis, impact assessment, resource estimation,
hierarchical approval, multi-warehouse resource allocation,
resource movement tracking, and audit mechanisms within a
unified workflow. The proposed architecture combines multi-
modal disaster information with cloud services, optimization,
and blockchain-based traceability. The allocation stage uses a
quantum-inspired optimization service motivated by quantum
optimization approaches such as the Quantum Approximate
Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) [22]. The system is designed
as a classical implementation and does not claim quantum
computational advantage.
The proposed framework therefore differs from approaches
that focus only on UA V-edge optimization, blockchain com-
munication, earthquake management, transportation risk, post-
disaster UA V networks, or energy transactions [1], [3], [11],
[19], [21]. Instead, the proposed workflow connects disaster
intelligence with administrative approval, constrained multi-
warehouse allocation, resource movement tracking, and au-
ditable logistics in a common cloud-oriented environment.
II. PROPOSEDMETHODOLOGY
In the suggested model for disaster management, historical
data about the weather, past disasters, geographic information,
machine learning techniques, calculation of the demand for
emergency resources, quantum computing for the optimization
of resource allocation, and blockchain technology for the
tracking of such resources are integrated into one system.
In terms of methodology, the end-to-end approach is im-
plemented, whereby data from the environment are prepro-
cessed to obtain the necessary spatiotemporal features that
help predict the possibility of a disaster occurrence and its
impact. According to these predictions, the necessary amount
of emergency resources is calculated, and based on the need
and conditions, these resources are allocated from several
different warehouses.
The overall processing sequence can be represented as
Data Acquisition→Preprocessing
→Feature Engineering→Disaster Prediction
→Impact Estimation→Resource Demand
→Quantum Optimization→Approval
→Blockchain Tracking.

K. End-to-End Decision Process
The last component incorporates the results generated from
all previous components. The weather data and the past disas-
ters records are firstly converted into spatiotemporal features
through the preprocessing procedures defined in Eqs. (4)-(8).
The normalized features, calculated with the help of Eq. (9),
are fed to the machine learning model. The disaster class
probability is computed using Eq. (10), whereas the disaster
class prediction is derived from Eq. (11).
The prediction of the disaster probability is then fused with
the prediction of the number of population impacted and the
geographic area of impact using Eqs. (13) and (14). The
severity calculation using Eqs. (15) is then performed. Finally,
the severity score is used in the resource demand estimation
formula in Eq. (16).
The resource-demand vector thus generated from Eq. (17)
is checked against the warehouse inventory. The warehouse
selection is done using Eq. (18), while the corresponding
allocation quantity is generated from Eq. (19). The allocation
thus generated is assessed using the formulation of shortage
given in Eq. (21) and the formulation of transportation cost
given in Eq. (22). All these terms constitute the optimization
objective as stated in Eq. (23).
The above optimization objective is represented using the
problem Hamiltonian given in Eq. (24). The QAOA-based
optimization algorithm produces the parameterized state using
Eq. (25), while the expected value of the optimization energy
is estimated using Eq. (26). The above allocation is passed
on to the resource request workflow, where the status of the
allocation is determined using Eq. (27).
Post the approval of the resource requests, the selected
transactions are logged using the blockchain tracking system.
The cryptographic link between successive transactions is
provided using Eq. (28).

III. RESULTS
The suggested disaster management system was developed
as a fully-fledged cloud-based solution that incorporates disas-
ter intelligence, resource-demand estimation, multi-warehouse
resource management, administrative coordination, operation
monitoring, and blockchain-based resource auditing. The sug-
gested system integrates the frontend, backend, database,
machine-learning service, optimization service, satellite-data
analysis, and blockchain audit service via separate but related
modules. The results obtained with the use of the implemented
system are shown in relation to system implementation, dis-
aster prediction, weather-data analysis, resource management
and allocation, and blockchain-based auditing.

H. Blockchain-Based Resource Audit
The blockchain audit layer provides an additional means
of verification of resource movement transactions. Instead
of substituting the operational database, the blockchain layer
includes the chained transactions and verifiable information
concerning the chosen transaction associated with the resource
movement.
The designed blockchain audit interface is illustrated
in Fig. 7. The current record is associated with Re-
source Movement ID 7 and includes one record within the
HARDHAT_LOCALblockchain network. The transaction is
marked as confirmed indicating the record of the transaction
within the blockchain.
Fig. 7: Blockchain-Based Resource Movement Audit Interface
Amongst some of the details provided in the audit inter-
face include the event type, reference identifier, blockchain
network, block number, recorded timestamp, entity identifier,
and transaction hash. In the given case, the event type is
RESOURCE_MOVEMENT, reference identifier is related to re-
source movement 7, and block number is 4.
The transaction hash in Fig. 7 acts as a unique cryptographic
identifier of the corresponding blockchain transaction. There is
an additional verification message that shows the fact that the
resource movement is linked to a corresponding blockchain
transaction. Hence, the blockchain audit layer increases trace-
ability of selected resource movements by adding a tamper-
proof transaction to the operational database.

IV. CONCLUSION
The proposed intelligent cloud-based disaster management
system incorporates multimodal disaster analysis, impact
estimation, emergency resource-demand estimation, multi-
warehouse resource allocation, administrative approval, oper-
ational tracking, and blockchain-based auditing into a single
framework. Disaster history data, meteorological data, and
satellite observation are employed for disaster intelligence
purposes as well as for connecting predicted disaster condi-
tions to emergency response planning. Cloud-oriented archi-
tecture consists of several layers including the frontend, back-
end, database, machine-learning service, optimization service,
satellite data integration, and blockchain audit as independent
yet interconnected layers, which make possible efficient infor-
mation exchange among all the stages of disaster management.
The integrated workflow unites the prediction of disas-
ters and planning the emergency response to them. Dis-
covered disasters conditions and impact estimation serve as
the foundation for estimation of demands of emergencies,
while warehouse availability, resource priorities, geographic
distance, and transportation needs are used as the base for
allocation of resources from multiple warehouses. Proposed
quantum-inspired optimization algorithm provides a classical
quantum-inspired formulation for generation of allocations
without claims that the algorithm could be run on quantum
hardware. Administrative approval stage and selection of the
transactions for recording them via blockchain are included in
the operational process, and the implemented results show the
feasibility of integrating disaster-intelligence, resource man-
agement, optimization, approvals, and auditable transactions
within the common platform.
Further development of the framework may include larger
and more diverse real-time datasets, better multimodal disaster
prediction models, and more detailed logistics considerations.
Predicting module can be assessed in terms of performance
with the help of commonly used quantitative measures and
by considering more types of disaster. Satellite data can be
utilized to make an evaluation of near-real-time spatial impacts
of disasters. As for the resource allocation module, it can be
upgraded with such features as vehicle capacity, transportation
duration, road network conditions, several disasters simultane-
ously and dynamic replenishment of warehouses. Evaluation
of the quantum- inspired optimization algorithm in comparison
